Law 2 of 4 · Gauss's law for magnetism

Magnetic field lines never start or stop. Every one is a closed loop.

Try to trap a north pole inside a surface. For every line that leaves, one comes back in through the magnet itself. Net magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero.

Try this

  1. Drag the surface over the north end only. Lines leave, but the same amount comes back through the inside of the magnet.
  2. Press Cut the magnet in half. You don't get a lone N and a lone S; you get two complete magnets.
  3. Keep cutting. Every piece, down to a single atom, still has both poles.
  4. Compare with Law 1: a surface around a lone charge had net flux. Here there's nothing to enclose.

What you're measuring

The rim shows where field crosses the surface. You will always see both colors. Lines that fan out from the north end sweep around, re-enter at the south end, and run back through the magnet to close the loop.

Why it matters

No one has ever found a magnetic monopole, despite dedicated searches. That's why magnetism comes from moving charge and spinning electrons. It is also why transformer cores and motor designs can treat magnetic flux like water in closed pipes: it circulates, and it never piles up.

About this simulation

Each magnet is modelled the way physics describes a permanent magnet: as a sheet of tiny currents along its long sides (the bound surface current). That makes the field divergence-free by construction, so lines close on themselves inside the magnet.
