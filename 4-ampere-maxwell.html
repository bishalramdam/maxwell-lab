Law 4 of 4 · Ampère–Maxwell law

Electric current, and a changing electric field, both make magnetic field circle around them

Ampère found that a current wraps itself in a magnetic field. Maxwell noticed that this fails inside a charging capacitor, where no current flows. His fix, the changing electric field term, is what later let him predict light.

Right-hand rule

Point your right thumb along the current. Your fingers curl the way the magnetic field circles. That's why the compasses line up in rings around each wire, and why two opposite currents cancel far away, the reason power cords run both wires side by side.

Maxwell's correction

Ampère's law says the circulation of B around a loop equals the current through any surface that loop bounds. Around a capacitor, a flat disk catches the wire's current but a bag that dips into the gap catches none. Maxwell resolved this in 1861–62: a changing electric field counts as current too.

Why it matters

Electromagnets, motors, MRI scanners and relays all rely on current making magnetic field. The displacement current term is quieter but bigger in consequence: with it, a changing E makes B, and by Faraday a changing B makes E. That loop sustains itself as a wave.
